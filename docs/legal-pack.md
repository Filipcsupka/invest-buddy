## 1. What separates informational screening from advice (sources)

### 1.1 Personal recommendation (investment advice)

- MiFID II Art. 4(1)(4), https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014L0065 (verified): "'investment advice' means the provision of personal recommendations to a client, either upon its request or at the initiative of the investment firm, in respect of one or more transactions relating to financial instruments".
- Delegated Reg. (EU) 2017/565 Art. 9, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32017R0565 (verified): a personal recommendation "shall be presented as suitable for that person, or shall be based on a consideration of the circumstances of that person" and recommends to "buy, sell, subscribe for, exchange, redeem, hold or underwrite a particular financial instrument". "A recommendation shall not be considered a personal recommendation if it is issued exclusively to the public."
- ESMA supervisory briefing on advice, ESMA35-43-3861, https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-07/ESMA35-43-3861_Supervisory_briefing_on_understanding_the_definition_of_advice_under_MiFID_II.pdf (verified):
  - "Publishing a list of 'best products' or 'funds of the month' would not, in itself, normally be regarded as investment advice. Assuming that such a communication is not addressed to a person as such but rather to the public in general (for example because it is publicly accessible on a website), it would normally not be presented as suitable for a particular person".
  - "a recommendation concerning financial instruments made through internet websites, investment apps, and/or social media (including through (f)influencers) could, in certain instances, be regarded as a personal recommendation and not as issued exclusively to the public."
  - On disclaimers: "even if a clear, prominent and understandable disclaimer is provided stating that no advice or recommendation is being given, a firm could still be viewed as having presented a recommendation as suitable for the client."
  - On targeting: "highlighting the particular personal circumstances that led the individual to be contacted ... is very likely to mean that the product is being presented as suitable for the particular investor." Content, language and tone count: "if the language is such that it strongly suggests an instrument, this will have an impact."
- CNB stanovisko RS2018-08 (CZ), https://www.cnb.cz/cs/dohled-financni-trh/legislativni-zakladna/stanoviska-k-regulaci-financniho-trhu/RS2018-08 (verified, Czech): informace poskytnuté "konkrétní osobě nebo osobám prostřednictvím distribučních kanálů (typicky internetu nebo elektronické korespondence) způsobem, který zákazník může vnímat jako doporučení vhodné právě pro něj ... naplní znaky investičního poradenství". Indirect suitability: "lidé Vám podobní si pořizují tento produkt" or "kdybych byl Vámi". Individualisation can also come from picking recipients by shared traits "jako složení portfolia, věková skupina". The opinion is addressed to securities dealers and intermediaries; whether it reaches a non-licensed publisher is a lawyer question.
- Slovak Act 566/2001 (NBS consolidated PDF), https://www.nbs.sk/_img/documents/_legislativa/_uplnezneniainych/z5662001.pdf (partly verified): "Investičným poradenstvom je poskytovanie osobného odporúčania klientovi na jeho žiadosť alebo na základe podnetu poskytovateľa investičnej služby v súvislosti s jedným alebo viacerými obchodmi s finančnými nástrojmi." (paragraph number not captured, probably § 6; the PDF may be an old consolidated version, and a 2025 amendment exists per a search hit, so the current text is **unverified**). § 54(1): a securities dealer provides investment services "na základe povolenia na poskytovanie investičných služieb udeleného Národnou bankou Slovenska" (verified in that PDF, current version unverified).

Reading: a public page that is the same for every visitor, uses no personal inputs and names no action is outside "personal recommendation" (ESMA, Art. 9 last sentence). The product's own ADR-001 rules (same alert for all, no profile or portfolio input, no sizing) line up with the factors ESMA and CNB name.

### 1.2 Investment recommendation under MAR (the wider test)

- MAR (EU) 596/2014 Art. 3(1)(34) and (35), https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014R0596 (verified):
  - (35) "'investment recommendations' means information recommending or suggesting an investment strategy, explicitly or implicitly, concerning one or several financial instruments or the issuers, including any opinion as to the present or future value or price of such instruments, intended for distribution channels or for the public."
  - (34)(ii) covers information "produced by persons other than those referred to in point (i), which directly proposes a particular investment decision in respect of a financial instrument". The persons in (i) are independent analysts, firms and anyone "whose main business is to produce investment recommendations", and the information there needs only to "directly or indirectly" express "a particular investment proposal".
- MAR Art. 20(1) (verified): "Persons who produce or disseminate investment recommendations or other information recommending or suggesting an investment strategy shall take reasonable care to ensure that such information is objectively presented, and to disclose their interests or indicate conflicts of interest concerning the financial instruments to which that information relates."
- Delegated Reg. (EU) 2016/958, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0958 (verified):
  - Art. 1(a) "expert": a person under 34(ii) "who repeatedly proposes investment decisions in respect of financial instruments and who: (i) presents himself as having financial expertise or experience; or (ii) puts forward his recommendation in such a way that other persons would reasonably believe he has financial expertise or experience".
  - Identity: persons "shall disclose clearly and prominently in all the recommendations they produce their identity".
  - Art. 5: disclose "all relationships and circumstances that may reasonably be expected to impair the objectivity of the recommendation, including interests or conflicts of interest"; for a natural person this also covers "any person closely associated with him".
  - Additional holdings article (article number not captured, probably Art. 6; text read): a person under 34(i) and an expert must state "if it owns a net long or short position exceeding the threshold of 0,5 % of the total issued share capital of the issuer" and whether long or short.
- MAR Art. 12(2)(d) (verified): market manipulation includes "the taking advantage of occasional or regular access to the traditional or electronic media by voicing an opinion about a financial instrument ... (or indirectly about its issuer) while having previously taken positions on that financial instrument ... and profiting subsequently from the impact of the opinions voiced on the price of that instrument ... without having simultaneously disclosed that conflict of interest to the public in a proper and effective way".
- ESMA statement on social media, https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma70-154-2780_esmas_statement_on_investment_recommendations_on_social_media.pdf (verified): other individuals who propose an investment strategy "could be also thought to produce an investment recommendation when the proposal is intended for wider distribution"; a frequent producer who "intends to reach a large audience, and presents him- or herself as having financial expertise" may be an expert with extra duties.
- ESMA finfluencer factsheet, https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-01/Finfluencers_factsheet_EN.pdf (verified): "Even sharing your opinion publicly about whether a share or crypto will go up or down – or promoting an investment strategy – can count as an investment recommendation"; "Disclaimers such as 'This is not investment advice' will not protect you in these cases"; "if you already invest in what you're posting about, or you could benefit if others buy it – say that too"; "Do not add pressure or create urgency".
- ESMA warning for people posting on social media, https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-02/ESMA74-1103241886-912_Warnings_on_Social_Media_and_Investment_Recommendations.pdf (search excerpt only, **unverified** in full).

Reading: MAR is the wider and riskier test. It has no "personal" requirement and says "implicitly" and "any opinion as to the present or future value". A published, rule-based list that says which companies "meet the conditions" can be read as an implicit suggestion, especially a list built for higher-risk "buy candidates" (list B). A disclaimer does not decide the question (ESMA, twice). Whether a screening output is an "opinion" or a neutral fact set is the central lawyer question.

### 1.4 Data protection, e-mail, cookies

- GDPR (EU) 2016/679, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679 (verified): Art. 6(1)(a) consent, (b) contract, (f) legitimate interests (text read). Art. 13 (read in part): controller identity, legitimate interests where used, "the recipients or categories of recipients", transfers to a third country, "the period for which the personal data will be stored", the rights to access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and portability, "the right to withdraw consent at any time", "the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority". Art. 21(2) to (3): objection to direct marketing, "explicitly brought to the attention of the data subject and ... presented clearly and separately". Art. 7(3) (as easy to withdraw as to give consent): not captured, **unverified**. Art. 28 processor contract with a mail and payment provider: **unverified**.
- ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC Art. 5(3) and 13(2), https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02002L0058-20091219 (verified): storing or reading information on a device needs consent, but "shall not prevent any technical storage or access ... as strictly necessary in order for the provider of an information society service explicitly requested by the subscriber or user to provide the service". Own-customer e-mail marketing for "similar products or services" needs an easy opt-out at collection and in every message.
- Slovak transposition of Art. 5(3) and 13 (Electronic Communications Act 452/2021 and the Slovak law on unsolicited messages): **unverified**.
- Stripe as processor, its DPA and US transfers: **unverified**, not read.

Reading: the language cookie falls under "strictly necessary ... explicitly requested" only if the owner agrees it is a user-requested function; the theme choice in local storage is the same class. Analytics are off, which keeps this simple. Current privacy page is missing the Art. 13 items for legal basis, retention of server logs, named recipients and transfers, and the right to withdraw consent. This pack adds them (section 3).

### 1.5 Consumer rules for a paid subscription

- Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32011L0083 (verified):
  - Art. 9(1): "the consumer shall have a period of 14 days to withdraw from a distance or off-premises contract, without giving any reason". For digital content not on a tangible medium the period runs from "the day of the conclusion of the contract" (Art. 9(2)(c)).
  - Art. 6(1)(e): "In the case of a contract of indeterminate duration or a contract containing a subscription, the total price shall include the total costs per billing period." Art. 6(1)(o): conditions for terminating a contract "of indeterminate duration or ... extended automatically". Art. 6(1)(h): withdrawal conditions and the model form.
  - Art. 8(2): the button must be labelled "only with the words 'order with obligation to pay' or a corresponding unambiguous formulation"; otherwise "the consumer shall not be bound by the contract or order".
  - Art. 14(3): after an early-start request the consumer "shall pay to the trader an amount which is in proportion to what has been provided".
  - Art. 16(m) (loss of withdrawal right for digital content after prior express consent and acknowledgement): the paragraph was not returned by the fetch, **unverified**; Art. 8(7) and 8(8) wording also **unverified**.
- Slovak consumer-protection rules for distance contracts and subscriptions, and Digital Content Directive 2019/770: **unverified**, not read.

### 2.2 Still risky

1. **The two horizon lists.** Core list (long-term) and list B (short-term, higher risk, owner decision 2026-10-17 "buy candidates for riskier investors"). Publishing a second list defined by risk appetite is the "group of persons" pattern CNB and ESMA name (a list aimed at a risk profile can read as "suitable for" that group). The internal name "buy candidates" is also an explicit proposal if it ever reaches a page. Keep list B owner-only until a lawyer answers Q1 and Q2.
2. **"Conditions met" wording.** "Setup met >=18" and "rules met" are rule-state labels, but MAR counts implicit suggestions and "any opinion as to the present or future value" (Art. 3(1)(35)). The word "setup" and a ranked list of names that meet the conditions can read as "these names are worth looking at". Needs a lawyer view on labels and ordering.
3. **Owner trading and MAR Art. 12(2)(d).** The owner holds the positions that the site scores. Voicing an opinion through a website while holding positions, without disclosing "simultaneously ... in a proper and effective way", is listed manipulation. Today disclosure sits on one page (portfolio). It is not next to each alert or company page, and it is not on Telegram. The disclosure must travel with the output. See COI text in section 3.6.
4. **Track record page.** Past signals with outcomes can look like a performance claim. Wording banned words are controlled in tests, but the page needs a lawyer read as a whole.
5. **Telegram alerts and e-mail digest.** A push message to subscribers about named stocks is closer to the "distribution channel" case than a web page. ESMA says apps and e-mail can be personal if personalised; they are not personalised here, but the check is the same for all recipients only as long as no preference or watchlist filter is added. Unverified how NBS or CNB would see a channel with a paying audience.
6. **Flag thresholds and "no signal for another purchase".** `portfolio.heldNoSignal` ("In portfolio, no signal for another purchase") talks about a purchase signal and sits in the public portfolio view. It reads as action wording even when ADVICE_OUTPUT is off. Candidate for a rewording to a neutral rule fact.
7. **Paid subscription for a screening feed.** Whether charging for the feed changes the analysis (a paid signal feed) is not settled in any source read. NBS and CNB treatment of paid feeds: not found.
8. **Operator identity.** Name, address, company id and contact are TODO (src/config/operator.ts). MAR identity disclosure and CRD Art. 6(1)(b),(c) both need them. Not launchable before they are filled.
9. **Third-party data licences.** Several providers forbid public display (docs/terms-requests.md). Separate from the questions below but blocks launch.
10. **Slovak and Czech text.** SK has had no native review (W23). CZ pages do not exist; CNB RS2018-08 is the nearest guidance for a CZ audience.
